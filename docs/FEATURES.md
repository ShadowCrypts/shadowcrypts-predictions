# Features, in detail

Everything the site does, how it does it, and what each piece was measured against. The [README](../README.md) has the short version.

- **Live data**: schedules, scores, injuries and DraftKings lines from ESPN's public feeds, cached in `.cache/`.
- **Forecasts**: once a betting line is posted, the forecast follows it with the bookmaker margin removed. In testing the line beat every rating model, so that's the honest best. Before a line is up, a pre-line forecast blends two rating systems:
  - **Elo** from results, with home field, rest, starting pitchers/goalies and injuries.
  - **Market-implied ratings**, learned from how past closing lines priced each team (and each MLB/NHL starter).
- **Who's playing (NBA)**: every player is rated from box scores (read again 72 hours after each game, in every league, because official scorers correct stats after the final whistle), and each game is adjusted for who's available against the lineups the team has been playing, from ESPN's injury report and current rosters. NBA teams heading east also pay a small time-zone penalty.
- **Projected scores**: every game gets a projected final score that agrees with the forecast. The margin is the posted spread where there is one, otherwise the win probability turned into points; the total is the posted over/under where there is one, otherwise a scoring model of team offense and defense (plus ballpark and rink effects). On held-out seasons the model's totals clearly beat the league average in the NFL and NBA and trail the posted total; in MLB and the NHL they're no better than the average, and the record page says so. Team pages show each team's offense and defense rating.
- **Player props (NBA, NHL, NFL)**: game pages project each player's stat line next to the sportsbook's prop lines from ESPN's feed (DraftKings now, ESPN BET for most of 2025). NBA: points, rebounds, assists and the rest from minutes (the lineup model) and per-minute production. NHL: points, goals, assists, shots, blocked shots and goalie saves from ice time, per-minute production and expected shots. NFL: passing, rushing and receiving from each player's share of his team's plays. Every league is graded against real lines (`lib/model/propEngines.ts` ties them together). The book was the more accurate number in all three, so prop chances follow it with its margin removed, plus the model's small fitted weight; lines where the model disagrees by 10+ points are tagged with how such lines have done in that league (profitable in the NHL so far, losing in the NBA, about even in the NFL). DraftKings posts NFL lines in this feed without prices, so those are read as the book's 50/50 point and never graded.
- **Props board and player pages**: `/props` lists every posted prop for a league's next games (search, stat filters, the model's tagged disagreements, parlay buttons, each player's average over his last five games next to the projection (also on game pages; `recentForm` in `lib/model/playerIndex.ts`, built once per model), his hit rate against the line over his last ten as a strip of games (L10, `lib/model/hitRate.ts`; `npm run backtest -- --hit-rates` shows it adds nothing to the book's chance, and the board says so with the numbers in `hitRateRecord.ts`), and where a line opened when the book has moved it since, with a sort for the lines that moved most). `/[league]/player/[id]` shows a player's injury-report status when he's on it, his next-game projections against his lines and his recent games against the line he was given (a chart per stat, with each game's line and his next one, and a table with averages), his last games against his next opponent, and has a link-preview image of its own (his headshot and team, his next game and its main lines with the chance of each over). Team pages list who's been playing, busiest first, each linked to his page; players carry ESPN headshots throughout (initials where ESPN has none). Before the books post, his page shows our projections next to his last-five and last-ten averages (never for a player ruled out), and his splits (this season, last season, last 10, home and road) are always there. Follow a player (the star on his page, stored in the browser) and the home page shows his next game and main lines under Your players (`/api/players/next`); while his team's game is on, his card and his page say so and link to the game page, where his stats so far and live chances are tracked. Every tagged disagreement is logged within a day of its game in `.cache/prop-ledger/` (kept 180 days) and graded after, a forward test shown on the props board and the record page.
- **Live on player pages, and better NBA minutes**: while a player's game is on, his page shows each of that game's lines with his stat so far, how far he is to the line and the live chance of the over (the game page's numbers; `gamePropBoard` in `lib/model/gamePropBoard.ts` builds a game's prop board for both). NBA minutes: `allocateMinutes` in `lib/model/lineups.ts` hands a short-handed roster's missing minutes out leaning toward the bench and takes a full roster's excess partly off the end of the bench (fitted on 2024-25 box scores and better on both later seasons: pre-line log loss 0.5868 to 0.5861, prop model 0.7262 to 0.7224), and upcoming games leave out roster players who haven't dressed all season and aren't on the injury report, since box scores, which the model learns from, list only players who dress.
- **Against the line**: team and game pages show each team's record against the closing spread (puck line, run line) and total, overall, over the last 10, and as favorite and underdog (`lib/model/trends.ts`; this season once a team has 5 lined games, else last season). Checked first: the team with the better record against the spread so far covered 51.2% of 3,901 past games, short of the 52.4% a -110 bet needs, and over/under streaks held 48.0%; both numbers sit under every table (`TREND_CHECK`).
- **Closing line value**: each logged prop pick keeps the line and prices it was first tagged at (`first` on `PropLedgerEntry`, kept from November 2, 2026), and once its game is played `gradeClv` checks whether the book's closing main line moved toward the model's side or away (picks tagged under an hour before the start aren't counted). The track record page reports it per league once 30 picks are in. Player pages also show L5 and an L10 hit-rate strip in the next-game table, and the props board can be narrowed to one game.
- **Playoff stakes and schedule strength**: the season simulation reports, for every game on the board, each team's playoff chance if it wins and if it loses (`GameStakes` in `lib/model/season.ts`). Game pages show it as "What's at stake" and the home brief names the biggest-stakes game of the next day and a half. Those games are played at their forecast without the run's random rating shift: splitting runs by a result whose odds depend on that shift counts a team's drawn strength as stakes (a test shows a 23-point fake swing on one game between average teams without it). Team pages say how hard the next five opponents are by Elo and where that ranks in the league (`lib/model/schedule.ts`), and show their next game's stakes in the header; league pages list the five biggest games ahead. Every simulation also saves each team's playoff and title odds for the day to `.cache/odds-history/` (`lib/model/oddsHistory.ts`, one reading a day, two seasons kept), and team pages chart a season's playoff chances once there are two days of them. Each team's outlook also says about how many wins (NHL: points) usually gets it in: the fewest final total that made the playoffs in at least half the simulations ending there (`TeamOutlook.cutoff`, from 30+ runs at that total).
- **How often it comes true, and sharing**: game pages say how often favorites at the pick's confidence have won in the track record (the calibration band holding it, 30+ games), and the props board charts the same for prop chances (`PropRecord.calibration`, every graded main line at the chance the board would have shown, pushes out). Pick'em records can be shared: `/picks/s/<yours-won>-<yours-lost>-<model-won>-<model-lost>` (`lib/picksShare.ts`; both records over the same games or it's refused) with its own preview image, and labeled as counted in the sharer's browser. The results page opens with a strip of the last 30 game days, colored by how many winners the forecast called. `npm run audit` also loads every page at 375px and fails if any is wider (an absolutely positioned label inside a table's scroller once widened phone pages; every `overflow-x-auto` is now positioned).
- **Turning points**: game pages, once a game is on or over, list the five scoring plays that swung the win chance most (`lib/model/turningPoints.ts`). Each is judged at its own moment with the live model: the chance with the score just before the play against just after, on the same clock, so time running off between plays isn't credited to them. Clock sports only. ESPN's hockey play clock counts up, so it's turned into time left first, and a play whose total score is lower than before (a shootout's own tally) is skipped; the game center now labels a regular-season NHL shootout "SO" (cache key `center3`). The game page's win-probability chart now draws a point after every scoring play on the game clock, with period breaks and a hover tip naming each play (`playFlow`, `PlayFlowChart.tsx`); baseball keeps the per-inning chart. The results page adds a comeback of the day: the winner whose chance fell lowest at a checkpoint (after a quarter, period or inning) during its game, 25% or less (`dayComeback`).
- **Data and API docs** (`/data`, linked first in the footer's Data column): every public JSON endpoint with its parameters and a live example link, the CSV and calendar files, and the rules (no key; `LIMITS.api` requests a minute, `LIMITS.expensive` for the calls that reach live feeds, `429` with `Retry-After`; no cross-origin browser access, so fetch from your own code). Every field it describes was checked against the live responses. Both 404 pages now offer Live, Props, Results and the track record, and the track record page has section links.
- **Parlays**: `/parlay` takes the legs you add (props or game winners, from any page or straight from its own player search, which lists a player's next-game lines with add buttons) and shows the real chance of cashing, fair odds, what the book pays and the expected return. Legs from different games multiply. Legs from the same game move together (a player's points with his points + rebounds + assists, a scorer's over with his team winning), so their joint chance comes from a Gaussian copula: correlations per kind of pair (same player, teammates, opponents, a player and his team's result, by stat), fitted on every graded game with prop lines (`lib/model/correlation.ts`, table in `lib/model/correlationTable.ts`). Fitted on the earlier half of games and tested on the later half, it put same-game parlays whose legs move together at 34% (NBA), 19% (NHL) and 36% (NFL) where they hit 34%, 19% and 35%; multiplying said 20%, 9% and 23%. Books price same-game parlays themselves, so you enter the price you're offered. The same correlations drive a "What if…" explorer on game pages before tip-off (`lib/model/whatIf.ts`): pick something that might happen (a player's over, a team winning) and every other line's chance is shown as it would be if it did, P(B | A) from the copula, with the player's own related lines listed apart.
- **Tracked parlays**: save a parlay and it's graded from the final box scores once its games end (`/api/legs`), with your record next to what the chances said to expect. While a game is on, each leg shows its stat so far (refreshed every minute from ESPN's live box score), a team-to-win leg its live chance, and a leg already decided says so: an over past its line has hit, an under past it has lost (yardage can still fall, so those wait for the final). Props also get a live chance to hit (`lib/model/liveProps.ts`): the pregame line and chance read as a whole distribution, plus the rest of the game at the pregame pace scaled to the time left, a little of a hot start carried over, and less left in a blowout; in the NFL it also leans with the score (a team behind throws more and runs less). Each parlay then shows its chance to cash now and what the ticket is worth at that chance. The live model was fitted and tested on players' stats rebuilt quarter by quarter and period by period from ESPN's play-by-play (`lib/playSplits.ts`, kept only where they add up to the box score; football from ESPN's per-play feed, which tags passer, rusher and receiver, with penalties, laterals, reversals and two-point tries counted the way official scorers count them): on later games it was far closer to what happened than the pregame chance at every break (halftime log loss NBA 0.685 → 0.526, NFL 0.689 → 0.561; NHL after two periods 0.458 → 0.359) and stayed calibrated, and leaning on the score beat leaving it out in the NFL. The same live chances run on every game page while the game is on (`lib/model/gameLiveProps.ts`): each player's prop table swaps its parlay buttons for his stat so far, a bar toward the line and the chance the over hits from here, for every player and stat, tracked or not. The sweat (`/api/sweat`, `lib/model/sweat.ts`) replays a parlay's chance to cash play by play through its games, while they're on or after, from the same play-by-play and the same live chances. Stored in the browser.
- **Live mode** (`/live`): every game on now, starting soon and just finished across all four leagues, with live win chances that glide as they change, your followed players whose games are on (each linked to his live props), and your tracked parlays sweating in real time: a ring with the chance to cash, each leg filling toward its line, confetti when a leg hits or the parlay cashes, and optional browser alerts for each (through the service worker, so they reach phones). A score ticker under the header (`/api/live`, one shared poll every 30 seconds while a tab is visible) runs on every page; it drifts like a broadcast ticker, pauses on hover and holds still for anyone who prefers less motion.
- **Fresh scores**: a league's model is rebuilt in the background every minute or two, so on its own a game under way could show a score a couple of minutes old. Everything that shows games on now (the ticker, live mode, game pages, the home board, parlay grading, alerts and `/api/predictions`) lays the latest scoreboard over the forecast (`lib/model/fresh.ts`; ESPN's scoreboard read again at most every 15 seconds, one read shared by everyone): status, clock and score, and the forecast graded the moment a game is final. The forecast itself, made before the game, never changes. The ticker and live mode refresh every 15 seconds while games are on (once a minute when none are), live game pages every 30.
- **Install the app**: an "Install the app" button where the browser offers one (Chrome, Edge, Android), and the Share → Add to Home Screen steps on iPhone and iPad, in the footer and next to the alert settings. Installed, it opens in its own window and, on iPhone, is the only way to get alerts with the site closed.
- **Background alerts**: turn alerts on (live mode, the tracked parlays, or your teams on the home page) and choose what to hear about: your parlays' legs as they hit or miss and the parlay when it cashes or busts, your followed teams' games about 30 minutes before they start (with the forecast) and when they're final (with whether the forecast called it), and your followed players' lines when the book first posts them for a game (with the chance of each over). They arrive with the site closed: the browser subscribes through its own push service (Web Push, VAPID; no account), your open parlays and followed teams are sent to the server whenever they change, and a scan inside the web server (`lib/push-server.ts`, started by `instrumentation.ts`) checks them every minute and sends what's due (`lib/push.ts` decides; nothing is announced twice, and nothing settled before alerts were on is announced late). The server keeps only the push address and what's being watched, drops a browser that hasn't visited in 30 days, and will only ever send to the browsers' own push services (Google, Mozilla, Apple, Microsoft), never another address. Needs the service worker, so production builds only; Safari on iPhone delivers web push only to the site added to the home screen. Where push isn't available, alerts come from the page while live mode is open.
- **Share a parlay**: the builder's Share button makes a link (`/parlay/s/…`, the legs' ids and the offered price) that opens a page and a link-preview image with the chance to cash. Every name, chance and price on it comes fresh from the model, never from the link, so an edited link can't put words or numbers in the site's mouth; legs whose games have started drop out. Friends can load it into their own builder in one tap.
- **Team colors throughout**: every game card, the game page hero, the home page spotlight, live mode, the matchup machine, power rankings and team and player pages are drawn in the teams' own colors: win bars split in both teams' colors, each team's glow in its corner, logos on discs of their color. `lib/teamColor.ts` keeps each color visible in both themes (falling back to a team's alternate color, or a lighter or darker shade of its own) and keeps two teams in one game apart. The home page leads with a spotlight game (the closest one on now, else the game to watch), and game pages lead with both teams' win chances as headline numbers over a key-numbers row (the pick, the line, the projected score, fair odds). The props board rings each player's photo in his team's color, shows how far the projection sits from the line and draws each over chance as a small bar; the record page leads with its accuracy as a ring beside how the forecast compares with its parts and the always-home baseline; the parlay builder shows its chance to cash as a ring, each leg with the player's photo and a chance bar. The results page leads with the day's hit rate as a ring and each league as a bar; the pick'em puts you and the model side by side as two rings, with each open game as a pair of team-colored tiles over the model's split; the player ratings open on a top-three podium; and the matchup machine is one versus card in both teams' colors, like a game page.
- **Motion**: pages fade and rise on navigation (React view transitions; the header and ticker hold still), the brand glow drifts slowly, and scores swell when they change. All of it switches off under the system's reduced-motion setting.
- **Any line**: player pages read the book's main line and its chance as a whole distribution and give the chance at any other line, like the alternate lines books sell (25+ points), with buttons to add them to a parlay. The spread around the book's line is fitted per stat (`lineSpread` in `lib/leagues.ts`). On later games the fit never saw, lines put at about 17% went over 15% (NBA), 16% (NHL) and 14% (NFL), and where the book posted alternate lines of its own, its prices and these chances were about even. A correction for overs hitting less often than the price says was tested and rejected: it showed up in one half of the data and not the other.
- **Live win probability**: during a game, the pregame forecast is updated with the score and the time left; finished and live games get a win-probability chart by quarter, period or inning.
- **Forecast movement**: each model build saves a reading of every upcoming game's forecast and line to `.cache/movement/` (only when it moved half a point, the line appeared, or 3 hours passed; kept 45 days). Each reading also keeps the key players the forecast counts as likely out (`keyAbsences` in `lib/model/injuryNews.ts`: half a chance or more of sitting, worth 8+ Elo), so the home page's biggest moves name the injury news behind them ("Injury news: Luka Doncic out") and followed teams get a push alert when a key player in their next two days' games is ruled out or back. Game pages chart how the number moved before kickoff. History starts when the app first runs, so a game needs two readings before the chart appears.
- **Track record**: every game since the warm-up season is graded against the forecast that would have been shown beforehand. Forecasts made within 48 hours of a game are also locked into `.cache/ledger/` and graded once the game ends, which gives a forward-tested record.
- **App**: a dark/light themed Next.js UI with a home page brief (game to watch, biggest favorite, closest call, biggest injury, the prop line moved most since it opened, hottest and coldest teams), yesterday's standout players, the day's biggest forecast moves and what caused them, your followed teams' next games up top with how each forecast changed since your last visit and their playoff chances with a win or a loss, game times in the viewer's own time zone, a filterable game board, a head-to-head against ESPN's own forecasts on the track record (both locked before each game and graded on the same games; forward only, since ESPN re-saves its past numbers: `lib/model/espnLedger.ts`), the short version of every coming forecast in plain words (what the line says, whether our ratings agree, what's behind their number, who's out, how far it has moved: `lib/model/explain.ts`), a sortable team stats table on each league page (results, plus what the box scores add: per-possession ratings, pace and true shooting in the NBA, shot share and PDO in the NHL, yards per play in the NFL, every team checked against ESPN's season totals), stat leaders on each players page (per game in the NBA, season totals in the NFL and NHL, the way each league's fans read them; checked against ESPN's own leaders), team pages (with scoring, defense, margin, last 10, home and road records and schedule faced, each ranked in the league), a tale of the tape on every game (scoring, defense, margin, last 10, road vs home, schedule faced, Elo and the opponent-adjusted ratings, each with its league rank), head-to-head history, a playoff machine (`/nfl/playoffs`, `/nba/playoffs`, `/nhl/playoffs`: pick the winner of any game, or have a team win out, and the season simulation runs again in the browser next to the plain run with the same luck, so what moves is what the picks moved; a scenario can be shared by its address), a matchup machine (`/matchup`: any two teams, any venue, plus best-of-7 odds and the same tale of the tape), a player comparison (`/nba/compare`, `/nfl/compare`, `/nhl/compare`: any two players stat by stat, next game projection and line, last 10, this season, how often each cleared his line, and how each did when they met, with a chart of their last ten games lined up by recency), NBA player and NFL quarterback ratings (`/nba/players`, `/nfl/players`) and an NHL player directory (`/nhl/players`), results for any date (`/results`, with the day's biggest upset, most confident call, standout players (the best line in each headline stat, from the box scores) and that day's logged prop picks graded, next to the overall forward record), pick'em against the model (`/picks`, stored in the browser), share images for every game, team and player, followed teams (stored in the browser), search for any team, player or page (Ctrl/⌘ K or `/`; about 2,400 NBA, NHL and NFL players with ESPN headshots, accents optional, regulars ranked above backups of the same name, games too (a team's next games, or the matchup when you type both teams), and the teams and players you follow listed first before you type; `lib/search.ts`, `lib/model/playerIndex.ts`), auto-refresh during live games and a sitemap.
- **Fantasy football** (`/nfl/fantasy`): every quarterback, back, receiver and tight end's projected points for the coming week, in PPR, half PPR or standard, next to his last three games and season average, with a range and any two players one tap from the comparison page. Touchdowns come from each player's own rate per target and carry, leaned toward the league's (`priorTouches`; it barely matters). The page publishes the backtest (`npm run backtest -- --fantasy`): a smaller average miss than the player's own season average in all four seasons on file and a better order within a week in three of them (2023 about even). Two things the backtest changed: the model ran about 15% high on the best backs and receivers, so `projectedPoints` applies a straight-line correction fitted on the seasons before 2026 (the miss shrank in 2026 too; quarterbacks came out right and are left alone), and mixing in the player's own average, which looked slightly better on the earlier seasons, was worse on 2026, so it isn't used. `npm run datacheck` holds team, position and byes to ESPN's rosters and schedule, and season lines to its game logs. Not compared with expert rankings; past-game projections know who was active.
- **Start or sit, and a forward test**: pick two players on the fantasy page or open them on `/nfl/compare` and it says who's projected more, and how often a player with that edge has outscored the other (52% at under a point apart, 70% at 4 to 6, 89% at 12 or more; every pair projected 5+ in the same week since 2023, and 2026 alone came out close). Each week's numbers are also saved before kickoff (`lib/model/fantasyLedger.ts`, `.cache/fantasy-ledger.json`, from November 5, 2026) and graded after, with no knowledge of who was active: someone expected to play who doesn't counts as scoring nothing. The page reports it whichever way it goes.
- **Standings** (`/nba/standings`, `/nfl/standings`, `/mlb/standings`, `/nhl/standings`): by division and by conference or league, from ESPN's standings feed, with home, road, last 10, streak and scoring margin, and each team's playoff chance from the simulation. ESPN's own order isn't the standings order (it can list a team 4.5 games back first), so `lib/standings.ts` sorts by record and uses the official seed for ties. The NHL gets its real wild-card layout: the top three in each division, then everyone else with the line after two, since a conference's top eight by points isn't the playoff field. `npm run datacheck` holds these records to the scoreboard's.
- **Injury reports** (`/nba/injuries` and the rest): ESPN's report, team by team, with what each absence takes off the team's rating for its next game, in Elo and in points of win chance, and the costliest in the league up top. A player long out costs little in the NBA, because the team's recent lineups have gone on without him. Past reports aren't kept, so what each status means in chance of sitting is an estimate, and the page says so.
- **On game pages too**: ESPN's own forecast next to ours, once it's been read before the game (`readEspnLedger`), and the weather forecast for outdoor games (ESPN's feed has no wind).
- **Backdrop**: the same ShadowCrypt artwork sits behind every page, fixed, so the page scrolls over it and it shows between the cards (which stay solid, so tables are untouched). It isn't a translucent layer: `scripts/make-backdrop.mjs` blends the image into each theme's own background color ahead of time, at the strongest level where the site's faintest small text still clears 4.5:1 contrast on every pixel. The near-white letters are toned down first so they don't hold the rest back. Dark is the image lightened onto the dark background (48 KB); light draws each mark in a deeper shade of its own color, a faint watermark (7 KB). Phones get a crop around the hood. A test holds the baked colors to the stylesheet's, so changing the theme means running the script again. It's hidden for print and for anyone whose system asks for more contrast. Lighthouse still measures text as the largest paint, not the image. Source images are in `assets/`.
- **Signature**: the owner's hooded "ShadowCrypt" mark signs the footer and both 404 pages (`app/components/shell/Signature.tsx`, `public/signature-360.webp` at 25 KB and `signature-720.webp` at 109 KB, plain files the proxy leaves alone).
- **Lean API**: Next.js compresses pages but sends route handlers' answers at full size, so the API compresses its own (`json`/`send` in `lib/http.ts`: brotli, else gzip): the player list goes from 189 KB to 38 KB, the live board polled every 15 seconds from 14 KB to under 3 KB.
- **Installable and offline**: a web app manifest with home-screen shortcuts, and a service worker (`public/sw.js`, production builds only) that keeps pages you've opened readable offline, with a banner saying so.
